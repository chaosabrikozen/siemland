SIEM review

A fixed-price review of your SIEM, in two parts.

Log ingestion

Are the right logs arriving, complete and on time? The review checks for the problems found most often:

  • Clocks out of step: sources without proper time sync (NTP) or with the wrong time zone, and the time an event happened confused with the time the SIEM received it.
  • Sources gone quiet: servers and devices that stopped sending logs. This is more serious than usually admitted: the rules that depend on them stop triggering, and nothing warns anyone.
  • Logs not parsed: events stored as raw text, or fields mapped wrongly, so rules cannot read them.
  • Gaps: important systems never connected, or connected only in part.

Detection rules

Do your rules catch real attacks, and stay quiet the rest of the time?

  • Silent rules that can never fire, because the data or field they depend on is missing.
  • Noisy rules that analysts learn to ignore.
  • Vendor default rules left untuned for your environment.
  • Coverage: which common attack techniques you can and cannot see, mapped to MITRE ATT&CK.

Industry research finds around one in five SIEM rules can never fire (CardinalOps, 2024).

You receive a written report with findings ranked by risk and a short list of fixes.

The review covers the SIEM itself. It does not cover incident response or your analysts' work.

AI-enhanced SIEM

A semi-automated SOC, built on your own Microsoft Sentinel and Defender. AI agents take the routine work, and your analysts keep every decision that matters.

Examples:

  • Triage and enrich new incidents before an analyst opens them.
  • Handle phishing emails reported by staff.
  • Flag noisy detection rules and propose fixes.
  • Warn when a log source goes quiet.

Everything runs in your tenant, under your control. There is no new platform to buy.
